Games & Feelings Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 28, 2026

Games & Feelings is a memory book for game night: you record what you played, who played, the scores, how it ended and how everyone felt. This policy describes Games & Feelings for Android, distributed through Google Play, and Games & Feelings for iPhone, iPad and Mac. The Android app keeps your library on the device you use, while Apple versions with iCloud sync also keep it in your private iCloud, so each has its own section.

On Android

The short version

Games & Feelings keeps your library on your device. There’s no Games & Feelings account or server, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and the Android app has no permission to use the internet. Games & Feelings never sends your games, players, scores or feelings to the developer.

What Games & Feelings keeps on your device

Games & Feelings stores these in its private app storage, which other apps can’t read:

  • the games and expansions you add, with any accent color, default result rule and star you choose;
  • the players you add: names, an optional emoji, a favorite piece color and stars;
  • each play you record: when it was played, the game and expansions, who played, scores, results, piece colors, the feelings exactly as you typed them, and an optional location and notes;
  • the feeling pictures you choose;
  • a last-known-good copy of your library as it was before your latest change, and exact copies of any library file the app couldn’t read, so a damaged save never erases your history;
  • the order you choose for each list.

A play you’re recording isn’t saved until you tap Save. Games & Feelings doesn’t add a lock or encryption of its own: anyone who can unlock your device and open the app can see your library.

Your library is about other people too: their names, scores and feelings. It stays on your device like everything else, and it leaves only when you share it.

Archives

In Data & backup, Export archive and Save to a file write your whole library, every game, player, play, score and feeling, to one file. It leaves Games & Feelings only for the place you choose, such as a folder or an app in the share sheet, and from there that place’s own terms apply. Anyone with the file can read your library, so keep it somewhere private.

Choose JSON archive reads a file you pick in Android’s file picker. Games & Feelings checks it on your device and shows you what it contains, and it replaces your library only after you confirm. The file you chose isn’t changed or sent anywhere. An archive exported from an Apple version with iCloud sync also holds that library’s conflicting and deleted versions; Games & Feelings for Android reads only the current library from it and doesn’t keep that history.

Score cards

When you share a play, Games & Feelings draws a picture of it. The game, the date and the results are always on the card. Names, feelings, scores, expansions and location appear only when they’re switched on; names, feelings and location start switched off. The picture leaves the app only for the place you choose.

Prepared archives and score cards wait in the app’s temporary storage, and Games & Feelings removes any older than a day the next time you share.

Backup and moving to a new device

Android can include your library and list orders in your device backup, and can copy them to a new device when you set it up. Games & Feelings allows cloud backup only when Android encrypts it end to end with your screen lock (PIN, pattern or password), so the developer can’t read it. Without that encryption, Games & Feelings is left out of cloud backup. A direct transfer to a new device goes from device to device rather than through a server. Prepared archives and score cards are never backed up or transferred.

You control backup in your device’s settings. To move a library between Android and iPhone, use an archive: the Android app doesn’t sync with other devices.

What Games & Feelings doesn’t do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No ads, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools, and no third-party code that collects data.
  • No selling or sharing of your information.
  • No internet access: the Android app doesn’t request the permission.
  • No access to your contacts, location, camera, microphone or photos.

Google Play and Android may give the developer statistics such as install counts, and crash or freeze reports from people who chose to share diagnostics with Google. Google provides those reports under its own privacy policy. Games & Feelings doesn’t write your library into logs or diagnostics.

Keeping and deleting your library

Your library stays on your device until you delete it. You can delete a play from its page, after a confirmation; it stays in the last-known-good copy until your next change. Restoring an archive replaces the whole library, and the library it replaces is kept on the device as the last-known-good copy. To remove everything Games & Feelings keeps on a device, clear its storage in Android settings or uninstall it. An encrypted backup in your Google account follows your Google backup settings.

Games & Feelings never sends your library to the developer, so there’s nothing for the developer to delete on a server.

On iPhone, iPad and Mac

The short version

On Apple devices, Games & Feelings keeps a local library that works offline. Versions with iCloud sync also send your library and its change history to your private iCloud database through Apple CloudKit, so your iPhone, iPad and Mac can stay in sync using the same Apple Account. There is no separate Games & Feelings account, developer-operated server, advertising, analytics or tracking. Your library is not sent to the developer. Older local-only builds do not sync.

What Games & Feelings keeps on your device

Games & Feelings keeps the same library as on Android in its private app storage: your games and expansions, players, plays, scores, results, piece colors, exact feelings, feeling pictures, locations and notes, with a last-known-good copy and exact copies of any file it couldn’t read. It also keeps your list orders. A play you’re recording isn’t saved until you tap Save.

Sync includes games, players, plays, exact feelings, scores, outcomes, colors, notes, locations, preferences stored with the library and import provenance. It runs while the app is open; offline changes wait on your device. Apple controls iCloud authentication, storage and protection. Android remains independent. Changing to a different iCloud account pauses sync instead of transferring the previous account’s library. List ordering and unsaved drafts stay on the device.

Archives and score cards

Export archive writes your library to one file. In versions with iCloud sync, it also includes retained conflict versions and deleted-record history; keep it private. A score card includes only the selected visible details. Exported files go to the place you choose in the share sheet. In sync-enabled versions, Choose JSON archive merges a file you review into the library, preserving newer records and retaining conflicts for review. Added records then participate in iCloud sync. Older local-only builds replace the library after confirmation.

Importing from a spreadsheet

If you choose a spreadsheet of past scores in Import from a spreadsheet, Games & Feelings reads it on your device and shows you the plays it found before adding anything. It keeps the plays you accept and a short receipt: when you accepted it, how many plays it added and a fingerprint of the file, so the same file isn’t added twice. The original spreadsheet file is not changed or uploaded. Accepted records and their provenance participate in iCloud sync in sync-enabled versions.

Backups

Your device’s backups can include what Games & Feelings keeps on the device. Depending on your settings, that includes iCloud Backup, a backup to a computer, and Time Machine on a Mac. Apple’s backup settings, including Advanced Data Protection for iCloud, decide how a backup is protected.

On a Mac

Games & Feelings runs in Apple’s App Sandbox. It reads files you choose in an Open window and its own app storage. Sync-enabled versions have outgoing network access for iCloud. The developer does not receive your library.

What Games & Feelings doesn’t do

  • No separate app account or developer-operated sync server; iCloud uses your system Apple Account.
  • No public database or automatic sharing with other people.
  • No ads, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools, and no third-party code.
  • No selling or sharing of your information.

Apple may give the developer statistics such as download counts, and crash reports from people who chose to share analytics with app developers. If you test Games & Feelings through TestFlight, feedback you choose to send, including screenshots, goes to the developer through Apple; never send a screenshot that shows something you’d rather keep private. Apple provides these under its own privacy policy.

Keeping and deleting your library

In sync-enabled versions, deleting a record hides it on your Apple devices after sync. Deleted records and conflicting versions remain in private iCloud and local history for recovery, with no automatic expiry, and are included in sync-aware archives. Deleting the app does not delete its iCloud data. You can manage app data through Apple’s iCloud storage settings; removing cloud history pauses sync on existing installations to avoid silently recreating it. On upgrade, the original local library remains as a safety copy. Deleting the app from iPhone or iPad removes its local storage; on Mac, moving the app to Trash leaves its data in your user Library. Exported archives and device backups follow their own storage settings. The developer holds no copy of your library to delete.

This website and questions

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. Visiting it is separate from using Games & Feelings: the app opens it in your browser only when you choose Privacy policy. GitHub may process your IP address, browser or device information and request details to operate its hosting. The site has no ads, analytics or scripts. GitHub explains how it handles website and account information in its Privacy Statement.

Questions go through public GitHub issues. Posting requires a GitHub account, and issues, your GitHub username and any attachments are public. Never post your library, an archive, or names, scores or feelings from it. The developer reads what you post in order to answer it. To have an issue removed, ask in that issue without repeating the information you want removed.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will appear at this address with a new date. Games & Feelings would describe any change that sends your library anywhere new here before it shipped.
